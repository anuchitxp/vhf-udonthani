One Modality · Four Levels of Depth

A spectrum, not a single scan

Bedside echocardiography spans targeted, binary assessments performed by the treating clinician all the way to exhaustive quantitative evaluation. Knowing each level's clinical scope, legal boundaries, and limitations prevents misdiagnosis and optimizes patient flow.

POCUS

Point-of-Care Ultrasound

POCUS — answer one question, right now

DEFINITION

Goal-directed bedside ultrasound performed by the treating provider to answer specific, binary clinical questions — not to produce a diagnostic study.

IN PRACTICE
  • ▸ "Is there a pericardial effusion?"
  • ▸ "Are there B-lines suggesting pulmonary edema?"
  • ▸ "Is the IVC collapsible?"
SCOPE — MULTI-ORGAN INTEGRATION
  • Cardiac windows — gross function, effusion, RV size
  • Lung ultrasound (LUS) — B-lines, pneumothorax, consolidation
  • IVC assessment — volume responsiveness / RAP estimate
  • Extended exam — abdomen (FAST), vascular access & DVT scans
KEY IDEA

POCUS is an augmentation of the physical exam — a hypothesis test, not a diagnosis engine.

Focused Cardiac Ultrasound

The 5 core questions of FoCUS

ASE + EACVI ENDORSED

A standardized subset of POCUS targeting only the cardiovascular system. Every FoCUS exam answers these five — nothing more.

1

LV Systolic Function

Gross categorization: normal · reduced · severely reduced.

2

RV Enlargement / Strain

RV size vs LV; signs of acute pressure overload.

3

Pericardial Effusion

Presence of fluid and tamponade physiology.

4

IVC Collapsibility

Volume status and right atrial pressure estimate.

5

Gross Valvular Catastrophe

Flail leaflet, massive regurgitation — the "big bads".

RULE-IN / RULE-OUT FoCUS classifies — it does not quantify. Severity grading belongs to comprehensive TTE.

Sonographer-Land · The Formal Studies

Limited Echo & Comprehensive TTE

Limited Echocardiogram

CPT 93308

Focused follow-up or disease-specific exam using a standard cart-based console, performed by a sonographer or cardiologist.

  • Re-evaluates a known abnormality or a single specific issue
  • Pericardial effusion drainage follow-up
  • Post-MI mechanical complications
  • Tracking EF recovery after revascularization
  • Detailed 2D + Doppler — but the full protocol is truncated

Comprehensive TTE

CPT 93306

The gold standard. Complete multi-window study with full 2D, M-mode, color, pulsed-wave, continuous-wave and tissue Doppler.

  • Quantitative chamber quantification
  • Detailed diastolic function grading
  • Valve severity: PISA · Vena Contracta · continuity equation
  • Hemodynamics: PASP, stroke volume & full calculations

⚠ Where Misdiagnosis Lives

Bedside traps — part one

◈  The "Pseudonormal" LV Pitfall

A hyperdynamic, underfilled LV (distributive or hypovolemic shock) can visually mimic normal or supranormal function while systemic cardiac output remains profoundly compromised. Always evaluate the IVC and chamber dimensions alongside LV ejection.

◈  Pericardial vs Pleural Fluid & Epicardial Fat — the PLAX Landmark

In the parasternal long axis, fluid tracking anterior to the descending thoracic aorta is pericardial; fluid posterior to the aorta is pleural. Epicardial fat pads sit anteriorly over the RV free wall, show mixed echogenicity, and move synchronously with the myocardium — unlike free-flowing effusion.

◈  Avoid Over-Calling RV Strain

RV dilation on A4C can be artifactually created by probe foreshortening. Ensure the apex is true; look for reproducible indicators — McConnell's sign, a D-shaped LV in short axis, and TAPSE.

⚠ Devices, Lung & Escalation

Bedside traps — part two

◈  Mind the Handheld's Acquisition Limits

Ultraportable probes sacrifice spatial resolution, temporal resolution, and CW Doppler. Never grade severe aortic stenosis or perform diastolic grading on a pocket device lacking calibrated CW alignment.

◈  Integrate Lung Ultrasound

In acute dyspnea, pair FoCUS with a quick 4-zone or 8-zone LUS: bilateral B-lines + LV dysfunction → cardiogenic pulmonary edema; isolated unilateral pattern → pneumonia or pneumothorax.

ESCALATION TRIGGER — CONVERT TO TTE

FoCUS is rule-in / rule-out — not definitive quantification. Escalate urgently for:

  • Unexplained shock with non-diagnostic windows
  • Suspected endocarditis — vegetations / abscess
  • Complex congenital lesions
  • Subtle prosthetic valve dysfunction

Key Takeaways

Scan with purpose. Know your limits.

1
Match the exam to the question

Binary bedside question → FoCUS. Quantification → comprehensive TTE. Never invert them.

2
Respect device physics

Pocket probes trade resolution for reach — no CW, no severity grading.

3
Integrate, don't isolate

LV + IVC + lung together defeat the pseudonormal trap and sharpen dyspnea diagnosis.

4
Escalate without ego

Endocarditis, prosthetic valves, unexplained shock — call for the comprehensive study.
